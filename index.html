Research Areas

The Gao laboratory is dedicated to exploring how plants achieve developmental plasticity to adapt to changing environments. Our central research question is to elucidate how plants integrate environmental signals — such as photoperiod, temperature, nutrient availability, and saline-alkaline stress — with endogenous developmental programs to precisely control their growth and development during critical transitions, including floral transition and inflorescence architecture.

Using rice and Arabidopsis as model systems, we systematically dissect the molecular mechanisms by which environmental signals regulate stem cell fate in the shoot apical meristem to control flowering time and floral organ development. Our ultimate goal is to provide new insights, innovative breeding strategies, and novel germplasm resources for precision improvement of crop flowering time and yield traits.

Our research integrates multidisciplinary approaches including crop genetics, cell biology, molecular biology, biochemistry, structural biology, and bioinformatics. We focus on three interconnected levels:

  • 1. Florigen synthesis, transport, and functional characterization of florigen activation complexes
  • 2. State transition of the shoot apical meristem and inflorescence morphogenesis
  • 3. Integration of multiple environmental signals in regulating flowering time and inflorescence architecture
